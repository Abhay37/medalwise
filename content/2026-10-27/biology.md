Subject: Biology
Exam style: NSEB (cell biology: meiosis, chromosome number vs DNA content)
Concept tested: Tracking chromosome number and DNA amount separately through S phase, meiosis I and anaphase II
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Haploid cell, but 12 chromosomes?
Question:
A plant has 2n = 12. A nucleus in G1 contains 6 pg of DNA. One cell is caught in ANAPHASE II of meiosis (before cytokinesis). What are the chromosome number and DNA content of this one cell?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 6 chromosomes, 3 pg
B) 6 chromosomes, 6 pg
C) 12 chromosomes, 6 pg
D) 12 chromosomes, 12 pg
Answer: C) 12 chromosomes, 6 pg
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
1. G1: 12 chromosomes, 6 pg (2C). After S phase: still 12 chromosomes, but DNA doubles to 12 pg (4C).
2. Meiosis I separates homologues: each cell gets 6 chromosomes (2 chromatids each), 6 pg (2C).
3. Anaphase II: sister chromatids split, and each chromatid now counts as a chromosome, so 6 x 2 = 12 chromosomes in the cell.
4. No DNA is made or lost yet (cytokinesis has not happened), so DNA stays 6 pg.
5. Answer: 12 chromosomes, 6 pg. Only after cytokinesis does each gamete get 6 chromosomes and 3 pg.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Students think "meiosis II = haploid" and pick 6 chromosomes (B), or halve the DNA too early (A, the final gamete value). D is the value for meiosis I (after S phase). Chromosome number is counted by centromeres, and it jumps back to 12 for a moment in anaphase II.
Caption (2-3 lines + "Comment your answer 👇"):
Meiosis II is the "haploid" division... so why can this cell show 12 chromosomes? 🔬
Count centromeres, not your assumptions.
