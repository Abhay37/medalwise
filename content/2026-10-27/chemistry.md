Subject: Chemistry
Exam style: NSEC (chemical kinetics, first-order gas reaction followed by total pressure)
Concept tested: Converting total pressure into reactant pressure for a gas reaction with Δn ≠ 0, then using half-lives of a first-order reaction
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): The pressure gauge hides the half-life.
Question: The first-order gas reaction A(g) → 2B(g) + C(g) starts with pure A at 200 mmHg in a closed rigid flask. After 30 min the total pressure is 500 mmHg. At the same temperature, what is the total pressure after 45 min?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 550 mmHg
B) 575 mmHg
C) 600 mmHg
D) 650 mmHg
Answer: A) 550 mmHg
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
If x mmHg of A reacts, total = (200 − x) + 2x + x = 200 + 2x.
At 30 min: 200 + 2x = 500 → x = 150, so A left = 50 = 200/4.
A fell to 1/4 in 30 min → 2 half-lives → t½ = 15 min.
45 min = 3 half-lives → A left = 200/8 = 25, so x = 175.
Total = 200 + 2(175) = 550 mmHg.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Students extrapolate the pressure rise linearly (300 mmHg in 30 min → 450 in 45 min → 650, option D), or treat 500 mmHg as the pressure of A. Others assume the reaction is over (600, option C). Only converting total pressure to A's pressure and using half-lives gives 550.
