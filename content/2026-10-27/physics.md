Subject: Physics
Exam style: NSEP (ray optics: refraction + reflection combined)
Concept tested: Apparent depth (real/apparent = n) applied twice with a plane mirror under water; the image of your own eye through a refracting layer

Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Look down into water. Where's your eye?

Question:
A tank is filled with water (n = 4/3) to a depth of 40 cm, and a plane mirror lies flat on its bottom. A student holds their eye 20 cm above the water surface and looks straight down (near-normal viewing). How far from the eye does the student see the image of their own eye?

Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 80 cm
B) 100 cm
C) 120 cm
D) 146.7 cm

Answer: B) 100 cm

Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
Seen from air, the mirror appears raised: apparent depth = 40 ÷ (4/3) = 30 cm below the surface.
So, optically, the eye is 20 + 30 = 50 cm in front of the mirror.
A plane mirror forms the image as far behind it as the object is in front: 50 cm, so the image is 50 + 50 = 100 cm from the eye.
(Check: the eye seen from inside the water is 20 × 4/3 = 26.7 cm above the surface, so it is 66.7 cm from the mirror and the image is 106.7 cm below the surface in water; seen from air that is 106.7 × 3/4 = 80 cm below the surface, which is 100 cm from the eye. ✔)

Why it's tricky (common trap):
Ignoring the water gives 2 × (20 + 40) = 120 cm (option C). Multiplying by n instead of dividing (thinking the water "stretches" the depth) gives 146.7 cm (option D). Students who do the physics right but measure from the water surface instead of the eye answer 80 cm (option A).
