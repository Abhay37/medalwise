Subject: Physics
Exam style: NSEP (thermal physics, calorimetry with phase change)
Concept tested: Heat-budget check before using the mixture formula: when the available heat can't melt all the ice, the final state is 0 °C with ice left over

Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Ice + warm water. Does all ice melt?

Question:
100 g of ice at −20 °C is dropped into 100 g of water at 40 °C in an insulated container of negligible heat capacity. How much ice remains when equilibrium is reached?
(c_water = 1 cal/g°C, c_ice = 0.5 cal/g°C, latent heat of fusion = 80 cal/g)

Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 0 g
B) 37.5 g
C) 50 g
D) 62.5 g

Answer: D) 62.5 g

Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
Heat the water can give while cooling to 0 °C: 100 × 1 × 40 = 4000 cal.
Heat needed just to warm the ice to 0 °C: 100 × 0.5 × 20 = 1000 cal.
That leaves 4000 − 1000 = 3000 cal for melting: 3000 ÷ 80 = 37.5 g melts.
Melting all 100 g would need 8000 cal, which isn't available, so the final temperature is exactly 0 °C.
Ice left = 100 − 37.5 = 62.5 g.

Why it's tricky (common trap):
Plugging straight into "heat lost = heat gained" with a final temperature T gives a negative T, so many students force answer A (all melts). Others forget to warm the ice from −20 °C first and melt 4000 ÷ 80 = 50 g (option C). Stopping at the melted mass (37.5 g) instead of the remaining mass gives option B.
