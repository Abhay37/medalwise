Subject: Biology
Exam style: NSEB (human physiology: breathing, dead space and alveolar ventilation)
Concept tested: Alveolar ventilation = breathing rate x (tidal volume - dead space); equal minute ventilation does not mean equal fresh air reaching the alveoli
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Same air in, same oxygen? Think again.
Question:
Anatomical dead space = 150 mL for both people.
Person X: 15 breaths/min, tidal volume 500 mL.
Person Y: 30 breaths/min, tidal volume 250 mL.
Both move 7.5 L of air per minute. Compare the fresh air reaching their alveoli each minute.
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) Equal for both
B) X gets 1.75 times as much as Y
C) Y gets more, because it breathes faster
D) X gets 2 times as much as Y
Answer: B) X gets 1.75 times as much as Y
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
1. The first 150 mL of every breath only fills the airways (trachea, bronchi) and never reaches the alveoli.
2. X: 15 x (500 - 150) = 15 x 350 = 5250 mL/min.
3. Y: 30 x (250 - 150) = 30 x 100 = 3000 mL/min.
4. 5250 / 3000 = 1.75, so X gets 1.75 times more fresh alveolar air.
5. That is why fast, shallow breathing (panting) is inefficient for gas exchange.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Minute ventilation is the same (7.5 L/min), so many pick A. Some just compare tidal volumes (500/250) and pick 2x (D). Others think faster breathing means more oxygen (C). The dead space is "wasted" once per breath, so more breaths waste more air.
