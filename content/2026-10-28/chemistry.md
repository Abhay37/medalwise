Subject: Chemistry
Exam style: NSEC (electrochemistry, combining electrode potentials)
Concept tested: Standard reduction potentials are intensive and cannot be added; combine half-reactions through ΔG° = −nFE°, which is additive
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Just add the voltages? Not so fast.
Question: Given E°(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = +0.77 V and E°(Fe²⁺/Fe) = −0.44 V at 25 °C. What is E°(Fe³⁺/Fe)?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) +0.33 V
B) +0.165 V
C) −0.037 V
D) −1.21 V
Answer: C) −0.037 V
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
E° values can't be added, but ΔG° = −nFE° can.
Fe³⁺ + e⁻ → Fe²⁺: ΔG°₁ = −1·F·(0.77)
Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Fe: ΔG°₂ = −2·F·(−0.44) = +0.88F
Fe³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Fe: ΔG°₃ = ΔG°₁ + ΔG°₂ = +0.11F
E°₃ = −ΔG°₃ / 3F = −0.11/3 ≈ −0.037 V
Why it's tricky (common trap): Most students simply add the two potentials (0.77 − 0.44 = +0.33 V, option A) or average them (option B). Electrode potential is an intensive property, so the number of electrons in each step must be weighted in through ΔG°.
