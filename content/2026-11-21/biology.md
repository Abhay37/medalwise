Subject: Biology
Exam style: NSEB (molecular biology: transcription + translation, reading frame)
Concept tested: Template strand -> mRNA (complementary, antiparallel), then translation starts at the first AUG (not at base 1) and stops at a stop codon that codes for no amino acid
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Decode this gene. How long is the protein?
Question:
The TEMPLATE strand of a short gene is:
3'- CGTACTTTGGGACTACCATT -5'
After transcription, the mRNA is translated by a ribosome. How many amino acids are in the polypeptide made?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 0
B) 1
C) 3
D) 4
Answer: C) 3
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
1. mRNA is complementary to the template (A->U, T->A, G->C, C->G): 5'- GCAUGAAACCCUGAUGGUAA -3'.
2. The ribosome scans from the 5' end and starts at the FIRST AUG (bases 3-5), not at base 1.
3. Reading in triplets: AUG (Met) - AAA (Lys) - CCC (Pro) - UGA (STOP).
4. A stop codon adds no amino acid, so the polypeptide is Met-Lys-Pro = 3 amino acids.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Treating the template itself as the mRNA (just T->U) gives no AUG at all, so 0 (A). Reading from base 1 (GCA, UGA...) hits a stop at once, so 1 (B). Counting the stop codon as an amino acid gives 4 (D). The second AUG later in the mRNA is never used, because the ribosome has already stopped.
