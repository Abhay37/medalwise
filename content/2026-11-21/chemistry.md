Subject: Chemistry
Exam style: NSEC (ionic equilibrium, solubility product and common-ion effect)
Concept tested: Ksp values can be compared directly only for salts of the same formula type; with a common ion, the ion's exponent in Ksp decides how strongly solubility is suppressed
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Smaller Ksp means less soluble? Check twice.
Question: Ksp(AgCl) = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ and Ksp(Ag₂CrO₄) = 1.1 × 10⁻¹² at 25 °C. Which salt has the higher molar solubility (i) in pure water, and (ii) in 0.01 M AgNO₃?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) (i) Ag₂CrO₄ (ii) Ag₂CrO₄
B) (i) AgCl (ii) AgCl
C) (i) Ag₂CrO₄ (ii) AgCl
D) (i) AgCl (ii) Ag₂CrO₄
Answer: C) (i) Ag₂CrO₄ (ii) AgCl
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
Pure water: AgCl s = √Ksp ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ M; Ag₂CrO₄: 4s³ = Ksp → s ≈ 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ M. Ag₂CrO₄ wins.
In 0.01 M Ag⁺: AgCl s = Ksp/[Ag⁺] = 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ M.
Ag₂CrO₄ s = Ksp/[Ag⁺]² = 1.1 × 10⁻¹²/10⁻⁴ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁸ M.
Now AgCl is more soluble: the squared [Ag⁺] crushes Ag₂CrO₄ harder.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Students compare Ksp directly and pick AgCl in water (options B or D), but the two salts have different formula types. Students who get part (i) right then assume the order stays the same in AgNO₃ (option A), missing that Ag⁺ appears squared in Ag₂CrO₄'s Ksp.
