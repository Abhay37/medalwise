Subject: Physics
Exam style: NSEP (gravitation, satellite orbits)
Concept tested: Total mechanical energy of a satellite in a circular orbit, E = −GMm/2r; shifting orbits costs the change in TOTAL energy (the KE falls as the PE rises), with GM = gR²

Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Raising a satellite: cheaper than you think!

Question:
A satellite of mass m is in a circular orbit of radius 2R around the Earth (R = Earth's radius, g = gravity at the surface). What is the minimum energy needed to shift it to a circular orbit of radius 3R?

Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) mgR/12
B) mgR/6
C) mgR/4
D) mgR

Answer: A) mgR/12

Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
In a circular orbit, KE = GMm/2r and PE = −GMm/r, so E = −GMm/2r.
Using GM = gR²: E = −mgR²/2r.
E(2R) = −mgR/4 and E(3R) = −mgR/6.
Energy needed = −mgR/6 − (−mgR/4) = mgR/12.
(The PE rises by mgR/6, but the satellite slows down in the higher orbit, which gives back mgR/12 of KE.)

Why it's tricky (common trap):
Counting only the rise in PE gives mgR/6 (option B), but the satellite doesn't need its old speed in the higher orbit. Using "mgh" with g reduced to g/4 at 2R gives mgR/4 (option C). Using mgh with surface g (h = R) gives mgR (option D): g isn't constant over such distances.
