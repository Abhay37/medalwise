Subject: Chemistry
Exam style: NSEC (thermochemistry, enthalpy of neutralisation with a limiting reagent)
Concept tested: Enthalpy of neutralisation is per mole of WATER formed (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O), so count H⁺ from a diprotic acid and find which ion is limiting
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Acid in excess, heat in question.
Question: 100 mL of 0.5 M H₂SO₄ is mixed with 200 mL of 0.4 M NaOH. The enthalpy of neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong base is −57.1 kJ per mole of water formed. How much heat is released?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 2.86 kJ
B) 4.57 kJ
C) 5.71 kJ
D) 11.42 kJ
Answer: B) 4.57 kJ
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
H₂SO₄ is diprotic: H⁺ = 0.100 L × 0.5 M × 2 = 0.10 mol.
OH⁻ = 0.200 L × 0.4 M = 0.08 mol.
OH⁻ is the limiting ion, so only 0.08 mol H₂O forms (0.02 mol H⁺ is left over).
Heat = 0.08 × 57.1 = 4.57 kJ released.
Why it's tricky (common trap): Students who use moles of H₂SO₄ (0.05) get 2.86 kJ (A). Those who count H⁺ correctly but forget the limiting reagent get 5.71 kJ (C). Applying the factor of 2 for H₂SO₄ twice (0.20 mol H⁺) gives 11.42 kJ (D). Heat depends only on the water actually formed.
