Subject: Physics
Exam style: NSEP (fluid mechanics: buoyancy in an accelerating frame)
Concept tested: In a vessel accelerating upward, both weight and buoyancy scale with effective gravity (g + a); the string tension on a submerged block grows too

Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Accelerate the tank. Does buoyancy change?

Question:
A wooden block (density 600 kg/m³, volume 1000 cm³) is held completely under water by a string tied to the bottom of a tank. The tank is in a lift accelerating UPWARD at 5 m/s². Find the tension in the string. (Water density 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)

Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 2 N
B) 4 N
C) 6 N
D) 15 N

Answer: C) 6 N

Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
In the lift's frame, effective gravity g_eff = g + a = 15 m/s² (downward).
The water's pressure gradient follows g_eff, so buoyancy = ρ_w V g_eff = 1000 × 10⁻³ × 15 = 15 N.
Weight in that frame = ρ_b V g_eff = 600 × 10⁻³ × 15 = 9 N.
Balance on the block: T = 15 − 9 = 6 N.
(Same as (ρ_w − ρ_b) V (g + a) = 400 × 10⁻³ × 15.)

Why it's tricky (common trap):
Many students assume buoyancy is "fixed" and ignore the lift, getting 4 N (option B). Others subtract the acceleration (g − a) and get 2 N (option A). Taking the buoyant force alone, without subtracting the block's weight, gives 15 N (option D).
