Subject: Biology
Exam style: NSEB (plant/cell respiration: respirometer and respiratory quotient)
Concept tested: Reading a two-flask respirometer (KOH flask gives O2 used; water flask gives O2 used minus CO2 released), then RQ = CO2 released / O2 used and linking RQ to the substrate
Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): Two flasks, one seed. What's it burning?
Question:
Germinating seeds are kept in two identical respirometers at the same temperature for 1 hour.
Flask 1 (with KOH, absorbs CO2): gas volume falls by 10 mL.
Flask 2 (with water instead of KOH): gas volume falls by 3 mL.
What is the RQ, and what are the seeds mainly respiring?
Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 0.3, proteins
B) 0.7, fats
C) 1.43, organic acids
D) 1.0, carbohydrates
Answer: B) 0.7, fats
Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
1. Flask 1: KOH absorbs all CO2, so the fall (10 mL) = O2 used.
2. Flask 2: CO2 stays in the air, so the fall = O2 used - CO2 released. 10 - CO2 = 3, so CO2 = 7 mL.
3. RQ = CO2 released / O2 used = 7 / 10 = 0.7.
4. RQ ~0.7 means fats (e.g. tripalmitin, RQ = 0.7). Carbohydrates give 1.0, proteins ~0.9, organic acids >1.
5. So these are likely oily seeds (castor, groundnut, mustard).
Why it's tricky (common trap): Students take the water flask reading (3 mL) as the CO2 released, giving 3/10 = 0.3 (A). Others flip the ratio, O2/CO2 = 10/7 = 1.43 (C). Many assume "seeds = starch = 1.0" (D) without calculating. Flask 2 shows the DIFFERENCE between O2 and CO2, not the CO2 itself.
