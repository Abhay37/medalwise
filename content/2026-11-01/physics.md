Subject: Physics
Exam style: NSEP (momentum + energy, two-body spring system)
Concept tested: At maximum compression both blocks share a common velocity; only the KE "lost" relative to the centre of mass goes into the spring (reduced mass)

Hook line (top of slide, <=8 words): The spring doesn't get all the energy!

Question:
Two blocks, each 2 kg, rest on a frictionless floor, joined by a light spring (k = 400 N/m) at its natural length. The left block is suddenly given a speed of 6 m/s towards the right block. What is the maximum compression of the spring?

Options (A-D, or "integer answer"):
A) 0.21 m
B) 0.30 m
C) 0.42 m
D) 0.60 m

Answer: B) 0.30 m

Solution (3-6 short lines, for next day's post/caption):
At maximum compression the blocks move together (no relative motion).
Momentum: 2 × 6 = (2 + 2)v, so v = 3 m/s.
KE before = ½ × 2 × 6² = 36 J; KE at max compression = ½ × 4 × 3² = 18 J.
Energy in spring = 36 − 18 = 18 J = ½ × 400 × x², so x² = 0.09 and x = 0.30 m.
(Shortcut: ½μv² with reduced mass μ = 1 kg gives 18 J.)

Why it's tricky (common trap):
Putting all 36 J into the spring (as if the right block were fixed to a wall) gives 0.42 m (option C). Setting the spring energy equal to one block's KE at the common speed (½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J) gives 0.21 m (option A). Doubling the correct answer because "both ends of the spring move" gives 0.60 m (option D).
